A proposal · Texas South Plains

Apiary-Keeper

One place to keep track of every yard, hive and inspection, built for a family-run apiary and made to work on a phone in the field, even with no signal.

Prepared by Adam Marquette October 2026 Draft for discussion · demo built and no agreements yet

The problems it would fix

  • History is scatteredNotes live in notebooks, phone photos and memory, so it is hard to look back at one hive's story.
  • Timing is easy to missInspections, mite treatments and feeding run on the season, across many yards at once.
  • Yield and cost are guessworkHoney, wax, nucs and queens are tracked apart from the colonies that produced them.
  • Paperwork is by handRegistrations, treatment records, labels and pollination contracts are all manual today.

What it would do

Grouped by area. The tag under each area says when it would arrive (see the phases below).

Yards and hives

First version
  • Each yard with its location, county, access notes and what forage is nearby.
  • The landowner for each yard: contact, the arrangement (honey, a fee, or free), nearby crops and who sprays.
  • A reminder to update BeeCheck (FieldWatch) when hives move.
  • Every hive with a tag you can scan, its box count, type and status.
  • Each queen: where she came from, her mark, when she went in, and when she was replaced.
  • A full family tree for every colony: splits, merges, swarm captures, requeens and moves. Nothing is ever erased.

Inspections in the field

First version
  • Record an inspection in under a minute with a few taps: queen or eggs seen, frames of brood, pattern, stores, temperament, disease signs, what you did.
  • Add phone photos, a short video or a voice note.
  • Works with no cell signal and catches up when you are back in range.
  • Walk a whole yard, scanning tags and quick-grading each hive.
  • Big buttons that work with gloves on, readable in the sun, simple enough for the kids to help.

Health and treatments

First version
  • Mite counts, noting the method (alcohol wash, sugar roll, sticky board).
  • Treatment records: product, label number, dose, dates in and out, who applied it.
  • Harvest from a treated hive is blocked until its withdrawal period is over.
  • Losses recorded with a suspected cause.

Planning the week

First version, weather second
  • Tasks for each hive or yard, with a due date and who is doing it.
  • Seasonal reminders made for you: spring build-up, swarm season, the flow, fall prep and winter.
  • A daily plan in driving order, with a packing list of feed, supers and treatments.
  • Weather for each yard from the National Weather Service, so a hail or freeze warning moves the plan.

Family group chat

Second version
  • A helper in your family's Telegram group that posts the day's tasks, overdue treatments, harvest totals and new orders.
  • Mark a task done, or send a photo of a hive by its tag number, right from the chat.

Honey, stock and sales

Second version
  • Each harvest tied to the hives it came from: weight, moisture and floral source, grouped into lots you can trace.
  • Stock of honey, wax, nucs, packages, queens, equipment, feed and treatments, with reorder reminders.
  • Customers, orders and invoices, with wholesale and retail prices and nuc and queen pre-orders.
  • Works with Square or Shopify for payments, and exports to QuickBooks for the books.

Your own shop website

Second version
  • A public site in your branding with what is in stock right now: honey by floral source, comb, wax, nucs and queens.
  • Orders and pre-orders for pickup, local delivery or market pickup, paid through Square or Shopify. The site never sees card numbers.
  • Customer accounts with order history and pickup reminders by email.
  • Contact and swarm-removal request forms that come straight to you. Yards are shown by county only, never exact locations.

Paperwork and reports

Second version
  • Texas registration and interstate permit renewals tracked, with a reminder before Sept 1, plus the health certificate for bees you bring into Texas and the optional paid inspection.
  • The health certificate or affidavit attached to each sale of bees, nucs or queens.
  • A check that each hive carries its permanent mark, or the yard has a contact sign, as Texas requires.
  • Honey labels that follow current Texas rules.
  • A prompt to report to the Texas Apiary Inspection Service when you note a sign of American foulbrood, Tropilaelaps mites or the yellow-legged hornet. It never diagnoses; you decide.
  • Treatment records you can export.
  • Reports on survival, yield per hive and yard, cost per hive and margin per product.

Every rule is checked against the Texas Apiary Inspection Service before it is built.

Pollination contracts

Third version
  • Contracts with each grower: crop, hive count, minimum strength, rate per hive and dates.
  • Which hives are at which orchard, with move-in and move-out dates.
  • Strength reports and invoices for growers, and a page where growers can check their placements.

An assistant, later

Fourth version · optional
  • Ask questions in the family chat, like "which yards lost the most hives last winter?"
  • Speak an inspection out loud and have it written up for you.
  • It only ever suggests. A person approves anything that touches money, sales, customer messages or regulatory records.

Swarm and bee removals

Optional · off unless you want it
  • Removal requests, quotes, scheduling, before and after photos, and the rescued colony added to your hives.
  • Checks that your Texas registration and removal statement are current before a job is booked.

Using it day to day

  • On your phone, in the yard. It opens in the phone's web browser, on iPhone or Android, and can be added to the home screen like an app. There is nothing to download from an app store.
  • On a computer, at home. The same app shows the bigger picture: every yard, the week ahead, harvests and sales.
  • In the family group chat. Day-to-day reminders and quick updates where your family already talks.
  • Your business stays yours. Only people you invite can sign in, with a password or an existing Google account, using proven, industry-standard security. You can ask for a second step at login, for yourself or anyone you invite. It is backed up every night, and nothing is shared or sold.

$1–36 more a monthOnly with the assistant, depending on how much it is used. A monthly spending cap keeps it in check.

What it would not do

  • It is not accounting software. It sends your numbers to QuickBooks instead.
  • It does not diagnose disease. It keeps your observations and photos.
  • It does not take card numbers. Square or Shopify handles payment.
  • It does not plan migratory moves out of state, like almond pollination in California.
